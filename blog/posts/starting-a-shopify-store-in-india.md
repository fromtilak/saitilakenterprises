Setting up NavPeth on Shopify taught me more in a month than any course could have. Here's what actually mattered, written down so you can skip the parts I got wrong.

## Payments: Razorpay, not the default gateway

Shopify's default payment options don't work smoothly for Indian sellers taking UPI and cards from Indian customers. Razorpay integration is the standard fix, and it's not complicated to set up — but I didn't do it on day one, and lost a few early sales to a clunky checkout before switching over. If you're building for an Indian audience, set up Razorpay before you launch, not after.

## Digital products change the shipping conversation entirely

NavPeth sells digital products, which means no shipping, no packaging, no courier delays — the product delivers itself. If you're deciding between physical and digital products for a first store, digital removes an entire category of operational headaches: no inventory, no damaged-in-transit complaints, no "where's my order" messages. The tradeoff is that digital products live or die on trust and clarity — the customer needs to know exactly what they're getting before they pay, because there's no unboxing moment to win them over after.

## The Dawn theme is enough

I didn't need a paid theme. Shopify's free Dawn theme, set up properly with a clear brand system — consistent colours, a real tagline, product images that don't look like stock photos — did the job. Spending on a premium theme before you have your first ten sales is solving a problem you don't have yet.

## Marathi-language products were not a side idea — they were the point

Most digital product templates in the Indian market are built in English, for an English-first audience. Building specifically for Marathi-speaking sellers, freelancers, and homemakers wasn't a limitation — it turned out to be the actual competitive edge, because almost nobody else is doing it properly. If you're building for an Indian niche, don't assume English-first is the safe default. Sometimes the underserved language is the opportunity.

## What I'd tell myself on day one

Don't wait for the store to be "finished" before listing your first product. Get one product live, priced, and payable — then fix the rest while real customers are already able to buy. A perfect store with zero live products makes zero sales.
